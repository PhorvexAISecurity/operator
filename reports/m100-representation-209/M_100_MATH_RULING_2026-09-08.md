## 1. Ruling and scope

The representation consists of scoped states, supporting evidence and explicit reporting boundaries. It does not change the decision objective or action selection.

| Question | Ruling |
| --- | --- |
| Q1 — States | Classify the specified protected condition or outcome as `at-risk`, `already-lost`, or `unknown`. Separate action support from irreversibility. Preserve evidence of historical irreversible losses. |
| Q2 — Coverage | Report a weighted share over an explicitly defined reversible at-risk population only when its complete denominator and required weights are available. Otherwise report the share as unavailable. Show known losses, unresolved entries and unmeasured out-of-band scope alongside it. |
| Q3 — Recall | Record the trusted detector set at claim time, its configured basis and relevant capability information when known. Recall remains unmeasured; no percentage is inferred from configuration. |
| Q4 — Invariants | Lost and unknown entries receive no reported cover credit. Missing evidence never becomes zero. Current detector changes do not erase historical losses. Existing selection and score fields remain unchanged. |

The weighted formulation from the earlier report is retained with an explicit evidence condition. A fact-count percentage is not substituted for it. Counts are separate descriptive fields with their own units.

No state, loss count, unknown marker or reporting ratio enters `net`, marginal gain, objective weights, cost, grants, lambda updates, candidate generation, feasibility or selection. M-99 may consume the shared state definition under its own ticket; that consumption is not implemented by M-100.

## 3. Q1 — Scoped states and admissible evidence

### 3.1 Unit of representation

A state describes a specified assertion about a protected condition or outcome, within a tenant, asset and asset-version scope. Record the protected property and retain links to existing fact keys where applicable.

A current access condition and a historical disclosure are different assertions. For example, changing a credential may change current access without undoing an earlier disclosure. A loss linked to an `inMemory` fact does not automatically change that fact's existing optimizer meaning or make every memory-related condition lost.

The reporting representation may therefore retain a historical loss record even when no current recon fact expresses that outcome. It must not create a weighted optimizer fact to represent it.

### 3.2 State definitions

| State | Definition | Reporting treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `at-risk` | An established, representable current exposure within the declared reversible model, without a valid already-lost classification for that same assertion. Its classification must have a documented observation/structural basis and reversible-condition semantics. | Included in the declared at-risk denominator population, even if no action can currently cover it. |
| `already-lost` | A completed, evidence-supported irreversible loss of the specified protected asset, property or version. Irreversibility means that the completed protected outcome cannot be undone under the declared recovery semantics. | Excluded from reported representable, coverable and covered populations; retained as a known loss alongside coverage. |
| `unknown` | The available trusted observations or semantic binding do not establish the representation status for the specified assertion. This includes unavailable observation and unresolved classification. Entirely unobserved gains may have no fact instance at all. | No cover credit. Record the reason for known unresolved entries; separately mark unenumerated out-of-band scope as unmeasured. |

For the same scoped assertion, apply a valid historical loss classification first; otherwise use `at-risk` only when its positive conditions are established; otherwise use `unknown`. Absence of a loss witness alone is not the complete at-risk rule.

`at-risk` concerns the current reversible exposure. It does not assert that every possible future consequence of that exposure would be reversible. Temporary inability to act does not change this state. A known reversible fact with no supported covering action remains at-risk and is annotated as unsupported. If its reversible-condition semantics cannot be established, retain it as a known unresolved entry rather than silently dropping it.

Unknown is allowed on an existing fact instance. Unknown-kind annotations are also required where the model declares a relevant kind but lacks an applicable trusted observer. The two annotations answer different questions and do not enumerate all unknown attacks.

### 3.3 Action support is a separate attribute

For at-risk entries, report actionability separately, with applicable reasons such as:

- `coverable-now`;
- `policy-disabled`;
- `infeasible-this-tick`;
- `unsupported`;
- `actionability-unavailable`.

Reasons may be multiple when several mechanisms are blocked differently. `unsupported` means no covering mechanism is mapped in the inspected vocabulary; it is not an irreversibility classification. Neither policy changes nor feasibility vetoes may silently remove an unchanged at-risk entry from a declared all-at-risk reporting population.

The defender's mutation rollback status remains separate. `MutationKind = irreversible` or an empty `RollbackRef` is not evidence of an irreversible attacker gain.

### 3.4 Which vocabulary entries can be associated with a loss

E1 confirms that `credential`, `retrievalState`, `artifactProvenance` and `controlPlanePersistence` are excluded from modeled cover. It explicitly does not infer an already-lost state from that exclusion.

| Vocabulary entry | Admissible association with `already-lost` | Insufficient by itself |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `credential` | A qualifying completed loss of a specified protected property, supported by the event-to-outcome rule. Distinguish current credential validity, credential disclosure and downstream harm. | No credential-rotation action; a generic read/use event. |
| `retrievalState` | A qualifying completed disclosure of protected retrieval content, or another specified irreversible outcome supported by the rule. | The kind name; a retrieval access indicator without protected-outcome evidence. |
| `artifactProvenance` | A qualifying completed loss concerning the specified artifact/property, with evidence supporting the declared irreversibility semantics. | A provenance anomaly or recoverable integrity failure alone. |
| `controlPlanePersistence` | A qualifying completed loss of control/integrity for which the declared trustworthy recovery condition is not available. | Creation of a RoleBinding, CronJob, webhook or other persistence marker alone. |
| Other kinds in the implementation vocabulary | A linked, separately scoped irreversible outcome may be recorded when its binding and evidence meet the same rule. A cover for a current condition does not exclude a historical loss associated with it. | Assuming that the presence of any covering action proves that every related outcome is reversible. |

This table rules the admissibility conditions. It does not assert that the current detectors emit sufficient evidence. There is no approved rule that only the four cannot-help kinds can be associated with irreversible losses.

### 3.5 Loss evidence contract

A loss classification requires a declared, versioned event-to-protected-outcome rule and evidence satisfying all of the following:

1. **Provenance:** authenticated source identity and trust acceptance at ingestion are recorded. An unverified event `source` string is insufficient.
2. **Binding:** the event identifies the relevant tenant, asset/version and protected property. A propagated neighbor score or a target Boolean alone does not establish this binding.
3. **Occurrence:** the evidence establishes the completed unauthorized outcome required by the rule, rather than an attempt, prediction, dispatch or stage label.
4. **Irreversibility:** the rule identifies why this completed outcome meets the declared irreversible-loss semantics. It cannot use absence from a cover set as its proof.
5. **Reproducibility:** event references, event and ingestion times, rule version, trust-at-ingestion evidence and the classification reference are retained.

`AnomalyScore`, `pHold`, `stage`, urgency, projected stages, exposure scores and action-derived owner booleans cannot independently establish these conditions. A trusted event is not automatically a qualifying loss event.

`LossClasses` may implement this binding, but its entries must identify the required payload predicates and protected-outcome mapping, not only event names. DEV supplies the concrete event schemas and source binding; MATH checks conformity to this contract. Existing security semantics govern the outcome classification; M-100 assigns no existential severity class or weight.

An empty or incomplete map is permitted with `lossAssessment = unavailable` or `partial`. It means no classification is available for the unbound cases. It does not establish zero actual loss and does not remove previously supported loss records.

### 3.6 Historical persistence and asset summaries

A valid irreversible-loss record persists for its original protected assertion. Changing detector configuration, adding an action, changing policy, replacing a pod or omitting an old fact from a later snapshot does not undo that outcome. A replacement asset/version can have a different current state. Corrected evidence requires an explicit correction record with provenance; it is not described as recovery of an irreversible loss.

Preserve a stable loss identity and deduplicate multiple witnesses of the same scoped loss. Retention must support reconstruction; a transient event window or a fact key that disappears on rotation is insufficient by itself.

For an asset summary within the requested claim scope:

1. If a valid known loss applies to that scope, use `already-lost` as the primary label.
2. Otherwise, if relevant observation/classification is unresolved, trust metadata is missing, the trusted detector set is empty, or no positive at-risk basis exists, use `unknown`.
3. Otherwise use `at-risk` for the declared modeled scope.

The primary label is not the entire asset state. Retain at-risk entries, known-loss references and unresolved-scope flags together. A loss of one property does not mean every property is lost; one configured detector elsewhere does not resolve this asset's observation gaps. The permanent out-of-band annotation remains even for an asset labeled at-risk within the declared model.

## 4. Q2 — Coverage and denominator honesty

### 4.1 Frozen population and distinct weight domain

Fix a reporting scope and snapshot `(sigma, t)`. Snapshot completeness below means completeness of the declared modeled input population, not completeness of real attacker knowledge.

Define:

- `F_t`: deduplicated current fact assertions in that declared reporting inventory;
- `R_t`, `L_t`, `X_t`: entries of `F_t` classified as at-risk, already-lost and unknown, respectively;
- `O_t`: the verified domain of the existing engine final-weight map for this same snapshot;
- `K_t`: the union of modeled cover sets for the action basis being reported;
- `C_t`: the union of modeled cover sets of the eligible candidates, when the complete candidate snapshot is available.

The state classifier partitions known entries: `F_t = R_t union L_t union X_t`, with pairwise disjoint sets for the same assertion. Historical losses need not belong to current `F_t`; they remain in the separate loss record.

The weighted inventory contains at most one entry per original qualified recon fact key in this snapshot. Additional property annotations or loss references must not duplicate that entry, split its weight arbitrarily, or receive new weights. If the binding from an existing key to its reported protected assertion is ambiguous, retain the ambiguity as an unknown entry with its reason; do not manufacture a finer weighted population.

E1 does not establish `F_t = O_t` or `R_t` contained in `O_t`. A raw fact weight is not automatically the final objective weight. Do not infer a denominator from `coverageValue`, `coveredWeight`, selected cover keys or a returned decision alone.

For `S` contained in `O_t`, define the reporting measure using unchanged engine weights:

```math
\mu_t(S)=\sum_{f\in S}w_t(f),\qquad w_t(f)\geq 0.
```

The numeric inputs must be finite and their provenance verified. This ruling defines no extension of that measure to missing keys, unknown gains or historical losses lacking those weights.

### 4.2 Ruled weighted share

Let the reported covered set be `B_t = K_t intersection R_t`. Where all required weights and inputs exist:

```math
D_t=\mu_t(R_t),\qquad
N_t=\mu_t(B_t),\qquad
\operatorname{coverageShare}_t=\frac{N_t}{D_t}\quad\text{if }D_t>0.
```

`coverageShare` is a partial reporting function. Its percentage is available only if:

- the scope, classification and inventory snapshot are known and complete for the declared population;
- all cover sets needed for the stated action basis are available and refer to that snapshot;
- every member of `R_t` has a verified finite, nonnegative final weight under the same weighting basis;
- the denominator is strictly positive.

Otherwise use `present = false`, `value = null` and a specific reason such as `inventory-incomplete`, `cover-set-unavailable`, `snapshot-mismatch`, `missing-final-weights`, `invalid-weight`, `no-at-risk-facts`, or `zero-weight-denominator`. Missing scope or historical metadata also makes a reconstructed share unavailable.

Missing weights must not be set to zero, inferred from unrelated values, or hidden by silently restricting the denominator. No new weights are added to the optimizer to make the report computable. DEV supplies a read-only population/weight provenance binding; if unavailable, the unavailable representation is the required result.

The reported `covered` and `representable` amounts are each numbers only when their own inputs are available; otherwise they are null with an availability reason. A known empty population may have a zero amount while still producing no percentage. Clearly labeled known counts may be shown independently. There is no automatic fallback to a count-based percentage.

When `C_t` is available from the matching candidate snapshot, report `coverable = mu_t(C_t intersection R_t)` under the same weight-availability rules. For a selected/actuated basis drawn from that candidate snapshot:

```math
0\leq N_t\leq \operatorname{coverable}_t\leq D_t.
```

This follows from set inclusion and nonnegative weights. If a fallback or later action uses a different context, record its matching basis or mark the affected fields unavailable; do not compare incompatible snapshots.

### 4.3 Action basis, aggregation and interpretation

Use an explicit basis, `selected-modeled` or `actuated-modeled`. For the latter, include only actions recorded as actuated, using their modeled cover sets. Neither basis supplies independent confirmation of security effect. Retain E4's dispatch-only qualification where applicable.

A per-decision record and a fleet roll-up must identify their aggregation scope. Roll-ups use a deduplicated union with tenant/asset-qualified keys and compatible snapshots. Do not sum overlapping full-candidate cover values. `coverageValue` and marginal `coveredWeight` retain their existing, different meanings.

Every percentage carries the modeled scope, population definition, weighting basis, action basis, detector metadata and unmeasured-scope annotation. Weight accumulation uses a documented deterministic key order; display rounding must not change stored decision fields or turn a rounded 100% into a set-coverage predicate.

The ratio is neither a system-wide recall estimate nor an unconditional bound on realized coverage. Actions may affect unobserved gains, and effects on modeled gains may remain unconfirmed. No universal comparison follows without additional assumptions that the supplied evidence does not establish.

An increase in the ratio need not mean security improved. Discovering a historical loss and removing its entry from an at-risk-only denominator can increase the percentage without any new defensive effect. Comparisons must retain the population and evidence changes.

### 4.4 What is shown beside the ratio

| Annotation | Required semantics |
| --- | --- |
| `alreadyLost` | Known scoped loss records, deduplicated loss count, affected properties/versions and evidence references. A count is a count of recorded losses, not loss magnitude. |
| `lossHistoryStatus` | Whether the retained history is available, partial or unavailable. Available history does not prove that all real losses were detected. |
| `lossAssessment` | Whether the declared event/outcome bindings are available for the specified scope. An empty map is unavailable; incomplete bindings are partial. |
| `unknownEntries` / `unknownKinds` | Known unresolved entries or declared observation gaps, with reasons. Their counts do not estimate all unknown attacks. |
| `uncoverableEntries` | At-risk entries without a presently usable covering candidate, with actionability reasons. |
| `unrepresentable.outOfBand` | The fixed qualitative marker `unmeasured`. It is never assigned a fabricated count, weight or percentage. |

No total lost weight is required. A historical loss remains reportable even without an optimizer weight. There is no denominator of the form `representable + estimated unknown mass`, and no severity penalty is introduced.

If no loss is recorded, say “no loss recorded in the available evidence,” with the history/assessment limitations. Do not say “nothing lost.” Presence of known historical losses is independent of whether the current detector set is empty.

## 5. Q3 — Detector set and recall limitation

Preserve the language implementation's record-time metadata semantics:

- `claim.detectorSet`: canonical, sorted, deduplicated copy of the configured authenticated identities at claim time;
- `claim.detectorSetKnown`: distinguishes a known-empty configuration from missing metadata;
- `claim.recallCap`: `unknown` if the set is empty or not known; otherwise `detector-bounded`;
- detector-set basis: `configured-authenticated`;
- detector liveness and recall measurement: unmeasured unless separately supported by actual evidence.

`detector-bounded` identifies the observation boundary. It establishes neither a recall percentage nor positive recall. Installed detectors, source strings and configured identities are not proof of connectivity or relevant per-asset observation.

Where a versioned capability mapping is available, record applicable detector-capable kinds and unresolved kinds by scope. Label capability as capability, not as completed observation. If the mapping is unavailable, say so; do not fill it with a guessed class list. Existing trusted structural/physics observations may support modeled fact states, but cannot establish that no out-of-band gain occurred.

An untrusted event does not add a trusted identity, remove an observation gap or qualify as a loss witness. A past qualifying witness retains its recorded trust-at-ingestion evidence even if today's allowlist differs.

For a fleet with no trusted detector configured, the output must explicitly state that recall is unknown. Use configuration wording consistent with E4; do not assert measured connectivity. The owner-facing no-trusted-detector message must not become a reassuring percentage-only headline. Any recorded structural-model amounts still carry the empty detector set and unknown recall.

Historical records without claim metadata remain absent in persisted form. Read-time projections show unknown; they must not borrow today's detector set, build tag, states or weights to manufacture a historical claim.

## 6. Record and implementation contract

The following are required semantic fields. DEV may fit their nesting to the existing audit types without changing their meaning or existing wire fields.

| Record component | Required content |
| --- | --- |
| `claim` | Modeled scope; tenant/asset/property scope; snapshot reference; detector set and known flag; recall cap; configured basis; capability-map status/version where available; reporting build identity. |
| `coverageShare` | `unit = engine-final-weight`; population/version; basis; availability and reasons; covered/representable amounts; optional coverable amount; ratio when defined; known counts and explicit exclusions. |
| `factStates` | Fact/assertion key, protected scope, state, classification rule/version, evidence or reason, and separate actionability. |
| `alreadyLost` | Stable scoped loss records and evidence references; known-loss count when available; history and assessment status. |
| `assetState` | Primary label plus at-risk, known-loss and unresolved-scope components. |
| `unrepresentable` | Known unresolved scope and `outOfBand = unmeasured`; no numeric unknown mass. |

Use `Unknown` as the safe zero value if an enum is introduced. An absent historical field must not default to `AtRisk`. Loss-related records must distinguish evidence existence from measurement availability.

The runner/reporting layer reads immutable observation, weight, cover-set and evidence snapshots and appends the new fields after the existing decision/actuation path. Taking a reporting snapshot does not feed new states back to the solver. The returned decision alone is not assumed to carry every needed input. If a required snapshot cannot be obtained within the permitted scope, report the corresponding field unavailable and identify the source-binding limitation.

Do not alter `internal/decision`, `internal/actionset`, `internal/feasibility` or `internal/cost` arithmetic to satisfy reporting. Shared model types are permitted, but selection must not consume the representation fields. Review that the reporting code does not mutate shared maps, slices or existing records.

An import check may prohibit solver dependencies on a dedicated claim-classifier package. It must not prohibit the solver's existing use of shared model types. Import structure alone does not prove unchanged behavior; source/dataflow review and regression identity are both required.

## 7. Claim-language boundary and M-99 handoff

D-16's language work does not wait for this ruling. M-14's non-actuation gate and T2-01's confirmation requirement remain intact. The phrase `contained (modeled)` remains subject to the ticket's M-99-applied and T2-01-confirmed conditions and its stated scope.

No objective value, ratio, equality of weighted sums, successful dispatch or legacy containment enum authorizes that phrase by itself. With nonnegative weights, an uncovered zero-weight fact can coexist with 100% weighted coverage. If an existing gate requires all specified facts, check the required set against the corresponding cover/effect set; weighted equality is insufficient. Empty or unavailable denominators also supply no authorization.

A completed loss cannot be described as undone by a subsequent action. A separate claim about remaining reversible conditions must identify that narrower scope and retain the relevant known-loss annotation. This is a claim-scope requirement, not a new optimizer constraint or a rule to abandon protection of other properties.

**Definition supplied to M-99:**

> An assertion is `already-lost` only when retained admissible evidence establishes a completed irreversible loss of the specified protected asset, property or version. Unsupported remediation, temporary infeasibility and absence of a covering action do not establish that state. `at-risk` identifies an established current exposure within the declared reversible model; `unknown` identifies insufficient observation or classification evidence. These classifications remain scoped: a known loss does not automatically classify every other property of the asset as lost.

M-99 must reference this definition. Any selection consequence belongs to M-99 or another separately scoped change. M-100 supplies no budget suppression, mandatory action, existential weight or objective penalty.

## 9. Implementation handoff and completion boundary

DEV's representation submission must include:

1. The implementation SHA and a binding from these semantic fields to the actual audit/model types.
2. The full reporting population, snapshot/cover access path and final-weight provenance. If a numeric share is unavailable, demonstrate the required unavailable behavior and identify the missing binding.
3. Versioned fact-to-protected-assertion and event-to-loss mappings, including explicit unresolved cases. Empty bindings are permitted only with the corresponding unavailable assessment, not as evidence of zero loss.
4. Retained scoped loss history and trust-at-ingestion evidence, with deterministic replay behavior.
5. Required tests, a source/dataflow review demonstrating no selection change, and independent DEV review.
6. Conformance evidence for D-16 scope, M-14/T2-01 preservation, and the M-99 definition handoff.

This settles the mathematical representation and its behavior under insufficient evidence. The concrete event payloads, source bindings, merge/deployment status and test results remain implementation evidence to be supplied. Missing evidence has a ruled output; it is not permission to invent a number or a semantic mapping.

The ticket remains open until its complete definition of done is met, including language and representation delivery, independent review, math conformance, required documentation and the closing build tag. This document does not claim those steps have occurred.
