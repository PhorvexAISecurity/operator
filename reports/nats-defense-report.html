Section 01

The workload, how NATS is used, and the JetStream threat surface

The workload under defense is a NATS JetStream cluster — a three-node, RAFT-replicated message broker (nats-0, nats-1, nats-2) running as a Kubernetes StatefulSet on Amazon EKS. NATS is used as the durable messaging backbone an event-driven application relies on; its business payload here is the ORDERS stream — replicated across all three nodes (R=3), holding 9 persisted messages. Each broker keeps its slice of that state on its own EBS volume — so the state, not the pod, is the asset.

Vulnerabilities & attacks that could overshadow JetStream

The attack fired was a container-escape foothold — MITRE ATT&CK T1611 — on a broker node, delivered as a SecurityEvent at threat level critical. On a message broker, one foothold is disproportionately dangerous, because that node is three things at once:

  • Data

    Restricted stream data on disk. The node's volume holds ORDERS messages classed restricted (asset value 1.0) — one hop from exfiltration.

  • Lateral

    A pivot into the cluster. From one broker the attacker recons and pivots to the other two (recon→lateral).

  • Quorum

    A consensus member. The node votes in RAFT; a compromised member can attempt to stall or corrupt the replicated log.

The stateful trap — and why dwell matters

Classic MTD answers a compromise by rotating the pod away. On a stateful broker that is destructive — rotating discards the persisted stream, and dropping a node can break the 2-of-3 quorum. So naive defenders either can't act (fear of data loss) or rotate blindly and rarely hit the exact compromised node. Either way the attacker's dwell runs unbounded — it keeps reading restricted streams and pivoting until it wins. Bounding that dwell without destroying data is the whole problem.

Section 02

What Phorvex did

Phorvex sat calm before the attack (reactiveOnly — no proactive churn of a stateful broker). When the foothold signal arrived, the deterministic brain (p1-coverage) evaluated the response under its integrity laws and contained the intruder in ≈ 20 seconds — bounding the attacker's dwell to that window.

Section 04

Attacker dwell time · how it is measured

  • def

    Definition. Dwell = seconds the attacker held effective access, from the attack landing to Phorvex containing the foothold.

  • how

    How it's measured. The console stamps the fire time; a ground-truth watcher (real kubectl) marks the dwell the instant containment is observed — the fresh phorvex.quarantine event / NetworkPolicy, or the pod disappearing on a rotate. Wall-clock, not an estimate.

  • this run

    Contained. Attack on nats-0 → quarantine of nats-0 → containment = contained, dwell 19.06 s (dwellOngoing = false) — the ~19–20 s band set by the ~10 s tick + ~1 s event-driven reaction.

  • bounded?

    Only when the foothold is reached. Dwell is reported bounded only when the action hit the foothold pod itself — evicted (rotated away) or contained (isolated). Before the node-selection fix, the same attack isolated a clean neighbor (nats-2): the console correctly recorded that as foothold-active with an open-ended dwell, because the attacker on nats-0 was never contained.

Dwell is surfaced live in the dashboard Report tab (per-attack + a mean-dwell rollup) and in the CSV export (dwell_s, dwell_ongoing).

Scope & method — what is real, and what is modeled

  • The attack is a red-team detector signal — a SecurityEvent representing a container-escape foothold — not an exploited NATS CVE. What is real and measured is Phorvex's reaction: the decision, the quarantine, the preserved data and quorum, the dwell, and the cost, all captured live on EKS.
  • Dwell for Phorvex is measured; for "no defense" it is unbounded (bounded below by the ≥95s LANDED observations). The R6 dwell is a labeled qualitative counterfactual — a blind timer rarely evicts a targeted foothold — not a fabricated number.
  • Quarantine is a hold, not a blip — the contained node is isolated and kept out of the StatefulSet for forensics until lifted, which is why replica availability stays at 2/3 afterward.
  • Phorvex quarantines a neighbor, not the entry pod — the node with the highest lateral-recon coverage per dollar, under the quorum floor.
  • Figures are live — from the cluster and /api/attack-results; the cost model applies real AWS on-demand pricing to the measured outcome.
