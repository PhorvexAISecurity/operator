With Phorvex = operator footprint only ($26.03/yr) → savings $338.39/yr. Tick interval does not change these — same ~1–2 rotations, same brief 50% availability dip in both.

3 · Security — worst-case latency, not typical case

  • Every trial at both cadences ended DEFENDED — the foothold pod was rotated away.
  • 10 s: first containment [4, 12, 10] s (mean 8.7); worst-case wait for a decision = 10 s.
  • 5 s: first containment [6, 9, 4] s (mean 6.3); worst-case wait = 5 s — a tighter dwell guarantee.
  • Attacker never progressed past exploit before being rotated. The 5 s benefit is a halved worst-case latency ceiling, invisible on the average case.

The two cadences are statistically indistinguishable on every objective term. Every decision covers the same 3 recon facts at the same ECost=0.82 regardless of tick rate, so cadence does not enter the objective. Firing the engine twice as often does not produce higher-value moves — it only shortens the worst-case wait for the next one. (An earlier n=1 run showed a 2× gap; it was hazard-model noise and vanished over 3 trials.)

5 · Recommendation

  • Keep 10 s (default) for cost-sensitive workloads — defended every attack at the same cost as 5 s, 10 s worst-case latency.
  • Adopt 5 s only for tight dwell-SLA workloads — halves worst-case containment latency and raises decision confidence, at 2× operator tick work and an operator restart to change --base-interval.
  • The levers that actually change cost/coverage are maxMovesPerTick and antiOverRotationWindow, not the tick interval.
