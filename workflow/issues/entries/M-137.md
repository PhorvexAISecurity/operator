### M-137 · No scale envelope: per-tick cost is writes + an O(cluster) in-memory cache, zero measurements · **HIGH** · 🔴 LIVE (third pass, revised mechanism) · `CONTROL`
**What:** `CollectSnapshot` issues ten unpaginated cluster-wide `List` calls per tick (`ingest/kube.go` ~L91-493) on a 10s cadence, against a hard 4×interval tick deadline. **Live measurement (V11, 2026-08-21) revised the mechanism:** the reads are served by the controller-runtime informer cache (`runner.NewManager(mgr.GetClient(), …)` — cache-backed reads), so they do NOT hammer the apiserver: over an 8-minute window covering 90 ticks, the brain's `rest_client_requests_total` showed only **6 GETs** — but **254 PUTs (~2.8 per tick)**, i.e. the per-tick apiserver cost is status-write amplification, scaling linearly with tenants × tick rate. The cache also means the brain holds the entire cluster's pod/RBAC/workload graph **in memory**, unbounded and unscoped. And the core of the finding stands untouched: nobody has measured tick duration or footprint versus fleet size — every test to date ran on demo scale (≤5 nodes, tens of pods). The `durationMs` field is already in the tick log (observed 4–68ms here), so the instrumentation exists — the experiment doesn't.
**Fix:** a load-harness measurement (tick latency + RSS + PUT amplification vs pod/RBAC/tenant count at 1k/5k/25k pods), a published scale envelope, and pagination/label-selectors on the RBAC lists at minimum.
*Sources: third-party review feedback 2026-08-21, verified against `ingest/kube.go` at `v0.4.39` (ten Lists, not eight as the review stated — the RBAC graph alone is four); mechanism revised by V11 live measurement.*
