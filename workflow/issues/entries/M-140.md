### M-140 · Tenant-state resurrection: a recreated policy inherits the old tenant's λ, cost model, and belief prior · **HIGH** · 🔴 LIVE (third pass) · `CONTROL->ECONOMICS`
**What:** Per-tenant learned state is keyed by the tenant NAME string in three places — `costByTenant map[string]*cost.P1Cost` (the λ dual solver + RLS cost model + conformal bound, `coverage.go:37`), the killchain conjugate belief prior (`Killchain.Memory.Observe(pol.Tenant, …)`, `coverage.go:510` → `killchain/conjugate.go:102`), plus `pendingRotations` and the exposure clock (M-129). `Manager.Remove` (`manager.go:190`) deletes the policy object and resets **metrics only** — none of the four state stores is pruned, and no decision-side Remove/Reset/Forget exists at all.
**The three sub-claims, kept distinct because they have different truth values:**
1. *State persists across a policy PATCH (same tenant key)* — **live-observed** (the V4 false null: the recycled `pxverify` tenant carried λ≈1.05 into the budget-0 test and silently suppressed all selection until a fresh-tenant re-test exposed it). This is **not a defect** — same tenant, continuous learning, λ carries forward is the design working as intended. It is a **testing hazard**: any experiment that reuses a tenant key inherits its economics.
   *Attribution caveat (2026-08-25, M-144 mechanism):* λ≈1.05 is only reachable via binding-budget up-steps (demand>budget AND spent≥budget), and during the budget-0→3 test itself λ must have **decayed** 0.0167/tick (1.05→0 in ~63 ticks ≈ 10–16 min). "λ-suppression" can therefore explain only the *opening* of a long null — any zero-selection tail beyond the decay window needs another cause (e.g. no positive-coverage candidate, the arms-A/B shape). The raw V4 λ series does not survive (pre-harness observation; only the summary value remains), so the V4 attribution is hereby **UNVERIFIABLE from surviving evidence** — kept as the cautionary tale, with the explanation downgraded from fact to plausible-for-a-bounded-window. This is why the campaign now captures everything to NDJSON.
2. *`Remove` prunes nothing, so delete/recreate also inherits* — **the defect, now LIVE-PROVEN (V8, 2026-08-21).** Rig: fresh tenant `pxv1t` seeded `lambda: "5.0"` (suppressed: 0/18 selections while the positive-control tenant `pxv2t` at `"0.1"` selected 12/30); then the standard operator gesture — `kubectl delete phorvexpolicy` + recreate the identical policy with `lambda: "0.1"`. Result: **0/24 selections post-recreate**, and the brain's own `phorvex_tenant_lambda{tenant="pxv1t"}` read **4.45** two minutes after the recreate — the bucket survived deletion and the new seed was ignored, confirmed three ways (behavior, instrument, code). The same gesture simultaneously proved the λ-seed inertness below.
3. *State lifetime is inconsistent* — it survives policy deletion but not a process restart. Same YAML, two behaviors, depending on whether the pod has bounced. The sharpest part, and the piece an operator can neither see nor predict.
**Made worse by a new feature (visible only at `v0.4.39`):** commit `40957f6` added a tenant-configurable λ seed (`policy.lambda`) — but the guard at `coverage.go:85-96` consumes the seed **only when the bucket is first created**; `costFor` returns early whenever `costByTenant[tenant]` already exists. So an operator who sets `lambda` expecting to reset the dual on policy re-apply gets a knob that is **silently inert in exactly the recreate case**, because `Remove` never deleted the bucket. The new feature doesn't just leave M-140 unfixed — it makes it more confusing, since the knob appears to work and doesn't.
**Fix:** in `Remove`, prune `costByTenant[tenant]`, the killchain memory, `pendingRotations`, and the exposure clock for that tenant — or key all four by policy UID so recreation is genuinely fresh. Until then, document that `policy.lambda` only takes effect on a tenant name the brain has never seen. Supersedes M-129's framing.
**Open question (for the tenancy-model owner, not a finding):** since the key is the tenant name string, two policies in different namespaces declaring the same `spec.tenant` share one λ, one RLS cost model, and one belief prior. For a tenant that legitimately spans namespaces that is presumably correct ("one tenant, one economic unit" — the `coverage.go:37` comment shows tenant isolation was the design intent). But it also means one namespace's budget pressure prices another's decisions, and a compromised workload in namespace A can shift selection in namespace B through the shared dual. Design or accident is not determinable from the code; it should be answered, documented, and ideally validated (warn on cross-namespace tenant collisions).
*Sources: third-party review feedback 2026-08-21, verified line-by-line at `v0.4.39`; sub-claim (1) live-observed via the V4/V7 confound, (2) live-proven by V8, (3) static-only.*
