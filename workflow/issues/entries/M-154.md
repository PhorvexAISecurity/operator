### M-154 · Audit retention is size-capped, not time-capped — decision-trace.v3's larger records halved the observable history depth, and the pre-v3 corpus rotated out unremarked · **MEDIUM** · 🔴 LIVE · `AUDIT`
**What:** the brain's audit log rotates at 5 × ~255 MB (`live.jsonl` + 4 rolled files ≈ 1.27 GB total), a *byte* cap with no time floor. At v2 record sizes (~64 KB fleet mean) the window held ~20k records ≈ 4.7 h of fleet history; at v3 sizes (~83 KB mean, 87 KB median at `0.4.80`) the same cap holds ~13–15k records — **roughly half the time-depth** at comparable fleet rates. Observed live (2026-09-04, T0-C L3): the entire pre-`0.4.80` corpus (0.4.73/0.4.74/0.4.75 records, present in the morning baseline scan) rotated out within ~90 minutes of the v3 brain coming up — mid-verification, silently. Any "100% of the retained corpus" claim is therefore only as old as ~2 h, and cross-build comparisons die with the rotation. Consequence compounds with the D-4 size amendment (v3 records are +26% on ~120-fact assets by design — the bytes are ticket-mandated, so the cap must move, not the record).
**Fix:** raise the PVC/rotation cap (e.g. 5 × 1 GB), switch rotation to time-based with a minimum-depth floor, or compress rolled files (JSONL gzips ~18×); until then, freeze evidence by export at verification time, never by relying on the live window.
*Sources: T0-C L3 verification (K3, `fixes/T0-C/evidence/live-l3/l3-verdict-0.4.80.txt` + addendum; baseline `fixes/T0-C/evidence/live-baseline/00-facts.md` Fact 4); live at `0.4.80`.*
